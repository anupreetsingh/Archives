## Punctuations

Punctuation marks are symbols used in writing to make meaning clearer.

- `.` Period: Ends a statement.
  - Example: `I finished my homework.`

- `?` Question Mark: Ends a direct question.
  - Example: `Where are you going?`

- `!` Exclamation Mark: Shows strong emotion or emphasis.
  - Example: `Watch out!`

- `,` Comma: Separates items, clauses, introductory words; Often creates pauses in a sentence. Example:

  - `I bought apples, rice, and milk.`
  - `I was tired, so I went to bed.`
  - `However, I decided to stay home`
  
- `;` Semicolon: Connects closely related independent clauses or separates complex list items. Example:
  - `I was tired; I went to bed early.`
  - `I visited Paris, France; Rome, Italy; and Tokyo, Japan.`

- `:` Colon: Introduces a list, explanation, or quote.  Example:
  - `Bring three things: a pen, paper, and ID.`
  - `He failed for one reason: he did not study.`
  - `The teacher said this: "Always check your work."`

- `'` Apostrophe: Shows possession or forms contractions.
  - Example: `John's book is on the table.`
  - Example: `Do not` becomes `don't`.

- `" "` Quotation Marks: Show exact speech, quoted text, or titles of short works.
  - Example: `She said, "I agree."`

- `' '` Single Quotation Marks: Show a quote inside another quote.
  - Example: `He said, "She called it 'perfect.'"`

- `-` Hyphen: Joins words or parts of words.
  - Example: `This is a well-known rule.`

- `-` Dash: Adds a strong break, interruption, or extra thought.
  - Example: `I had one goal - to pass.`

- `( )` Parentheses: Add extra information that is not essential to the main sentence.
  - Example: `He moved to Delhi (India's capital).`

- `[ ]` Brackets: Add clarification, correction, or extra information inside quoted text.
  - Example: `She said, "He [the teacher] helped me."`

- `{ }` Braces: Group items, especially in mathematics, programming, or technical writing.
  - Example: `Set A = {1, 2, 3}.`

- `...` Ellipsis: Shows omitted words, hesitation, or a trailing thought.
  - Example: `I thought about it... then I agreed.`

- `/` Slash: Shows alternatives, separates lines of poetry, or appears in dates and fractions.
  - Example: `You may choose yes/no.`

- `\` Backslash: Used mostly in computing, file paths, and escape characters.
  - Example: `C:\Users\Name`

- `*` Asterisk: Marks a footnote, correction, or special note.
  - Example: `Read the note at the bottom.*`

- `_` Underscore: Connects words in usernames, file names, or programming.
  - Example: `first_name`

## Compound Nouns

A compound noun is a noun made up of two or more words(one noun and the other a noun, adjective or verb) that together refer to a single concept.

Example: greenhouse, record-keeping, machine learning, swimming pool, snowman, etc
