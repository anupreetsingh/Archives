# French Alphabet and Accents

## Alphabet

French uses the same **26 letters** as English.

### Letter names and pronunciation

Use these names when reciting the alphabet or spelling a word aloud. A letter's **name** can differ from its **sound inside a word**: **h** is called **/aʃ/**, but it does not produce an English “h” sound in French words.

The English-style guides below are approximate. The **IPA** (International Phonetic Alphabet) column gives a more precise pronunciation; symbols between **/ /** represent sounds.

- **ay** represents a steady vowel: start saying English “say,” but stop before the vowel glides toward “ee.”
- **oh** is also steady, without the “w” glide often heard at the end of English “go.”
- **zh** is the sound in English “vision” or “measure.”
- **r** represents the French sound made toward the back of the mouth; see the tips below.

| --- | --- | --- |

| E e | /ə/ | uh, with lightly rounded lips |

#### Sounds that need special attention

**U /y/** has no exact equivalent in most English accents. Say **ee /i/**, keep your tongue in that position, and round your lips as if saying **oo**. This produces French **u**. By comparison, French **ou /u/** sounds approximately like English **oo** in “food.”

- **tu /ty/** (you): use French **u**.
- **tout /tu/** (all): use **ou**.
- The letter **Q /ky/** uses the same vowel as **U /y/**. Avoid pronouncing its name like English “cue.”

**R /ʁ/** is usually made with the back of the tongue near the uvula, the small hanging part at the back of the mouth. Let air pass with gentle friction; avoid forcing a harsh gargling sound. French **r** varies by region, but this is a useful starting point.

**E /ə/** is a short, relaxed vowel with lightly rounded lips. Its letter name is different from **é /e/** and **è /ɛ/**, explained under [accents](#accents).

**G and J** are easy to mix up if you know the English alphabet: French **G** is **zhay**, while French **J** is **zhee**. Neither begins with the English “j” sound in “jam.”

#### Practice: spelling a word

**Bonjour** is spelled **B – O – N – J – O – U – R**.

Say the letter names individually: **bay – oh – en – zhee – oh – French u – ehr**.

This is how you spell the word aloud. For the pronunciation of the complete word, see [essential expressions](French.md#essential-expressions).

## Accents

Accented forms such as **é** and **ç** are variations of these letters, not additional letters in the alphabet. French uses five main written marks, commonly taught together as accents. More precisely, these are **diacritics**: marks added to letters. They can guide pronunciation, distinguish words, or preserve older spellings. They are part of the spelling and remain on capital letters: **é → É**, **à → À**, **ç → Ç**.

An accent mark does **not** mean “stress this syllable.” Learn each mark as part of the word's spelling.

### Acute accent: accent aigu — é

The acute accent slopes upward to the right and appears only on **e**, producing **é**. It indicates **/e/**, the steady **ay** sound described in the alphabet guide.

**Été /ete/** (summer) is approximately **ay-tay**. Both instances of **é** have the same sound.

### Grave accent: accent grave — à, è, ù

The grave accent slopes downward to the right. Its effect depends on the letter:

| Form | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| è | Gives **/ɛ/**, like the vowel in English “bed.” | **mère /mɛʁ/** (mother), approximately **mehr** |
| à | Does not change the sound of **a**; distinguishes words. | **a** (has) and **à** (to / at), both **/a/** |
| ù | Does not change pronunciation in **où**; distinguishes it from **ou**. | **où** (where) and **ou** (or), both **/u/**, approximately **oo** |

**Il a un livre à la maison.** → He has a book at home. Here, **a** expresses possession, while **à** introduces the location.

### Circumflex: accent circonflexe — â, ê, î, ô, û

The circumflex looks like a small roof. It can affect pronunciation, distinguish words, or reflect a word's history.

| Form | Pronunciation guidance | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| â | Often pronounced like **a /a/** today; some accents preserve a farther-back **/ɑ/** sound. | **pâte** (paste / dough / pasta) |
| ê | Usually **/ɛ/**, the same vowel as **è**. | **être /ɛtʁ/** (to be) |
| î | Usually the same **/i/** sound as **i**. | **île /il/** (island), approximately **eel** |
| ô | Usually **/o/**, a closed, steady **oh** sound. | **hôtel /otɛl/** (hotel), approximately **oh-tell** |
| û | Usually the same **/y/** sound as **u**. | **sûr /syʁ/** (sure) |

The circumflex sometimes distinguishes words: **sur** (on) and **sûr** (sure) are normally pronounced the same. It can also mark a letter that disappeared from an older spelling, often **s**: compare **forêt** (forest) with English “forest.” Pronunciation distinctions involving the circumflex vary by region and word.

### Diaeresis: tréma — ë, ï, ü

The tréma is a pair of dots above a vowel. It usually signals that adjacent vowel letters should be read separately rather than as their usual combination. It does not assign one fixed sound to the marked letter.

- **Noël /nɔɛl/** (Christmas): approximately **no-ell**, with separate **o** and **e** vowel sounds.
- **Naïf /naif/** (naive): approximately **nah-eef**, with separate **a** and **i** vowel sounds. Without the dots, **ai** commonly represents a single **/ɛ/** sound.

It does not always create an extra syllable. In **aiguë** (sharp or high-pitched, feminine), the dots show that **u** must be pronounced; the final **e** remains silent.

### Cedilla: cédille — ç

The cedilla is the mark **under c**. It gives **c** an **/s/** sound before **a**, **o**, or **u**, where it would normally have a **/k/** sound.

| Word | Meaning | Effect of the cedilla |
| --- | --- | --- |
| français | French | **ç** sounds like **s** before **a**. |
| garçon | boy | **ç** sounds like **s** before **o**. |
| reçu | received | **ç** sounds like **s** before **u**. |

Before **e**, **i**, or **y**, **c** already normally has an **/s/** sound, so no cedilla is needed: **merci** (thank you).

### Naming accented letters when spelling

Say the base letter's name from the alphabet table, followed by the mark's French name:

| Written form | Say when spelling |
| --- | --- |
| é | **e accent aigu** |
| è | **e accent grave** |
| ê | **e accent circonflexe** |
| ë | **e tréma** |
| ç | **c cédille** |

For example, spell **été** as **e accent aigu – t – e accent aigu**. The marked vowel sounds like **/e/** when you pronounce the word, but you use the base letter name **E /ə/** when naming its spelling this way.
