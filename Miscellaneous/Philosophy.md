## Abstract

**To abstract** means to identify and isolate the core features relevant at this point of discussion while hiding the non-essential details that would add unnecessary complexity.

### Abstract Idea

An **Abstract idea** is a general concept formed by taking relevant common aspects and ignoring the differences between individual examples.

Example 1: When people discuss a chair as an abstract idea, they usually mean an inanimate object that people sit on, often with legs and a back. Details such as whether the chair is made of wood or metal, how its joints are built, or how it was manufactured are ignored because they are not essential to the idea being discussed.

Example 2: Justice is an abstract idea that entails people are treated according to principles of fairness and rights regardless of the specific details of individual situations, like sentencing a murderer, making a company pay its workers fairly, a refree enforces the rules equally, etc.

### Abstraction

The same applies when we use abstraction to explain a technical concept. We isolate the parts that matter at a particular level of understanding and hide the details that are not relevant at that level. Like using abstraction of components in OOP.

## Inductive vs Deductive Reasoning

Both are forms of reasoning used to infer(or arrive at) a conclusion from one or more premises.

### Inductive(To lead into) Reasoning

A bottom-up form of using specific scenarios and observed patterns to reach a generalized conclusion. The conclusion is probable, not guaranteed. Even if every observation so far supports it, a future observation could prove it false.
Example:

```text
The sun has risen every day since I have lived.
The sun will rise tomorrow.
```

### Deductive(To lead down) Reasoning

A top down approach of applying a general rule to a specific scenario to reach a conclusion that must be true if the rule is true and reasoning is logically valid. The conclusion in this case must be true.

Example:

```text
All humans are mortal.
Socrates is a human.
Therefore, Socrates is mortal.
```
